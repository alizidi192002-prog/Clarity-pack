What to Do With a Third Paycheck: A Simple Plan for Your Extra Payday

Updated 2026-10-17 · 6 min read

If you're paid every two weeks, most months bring two paychecks, but a couple of months each year bring three. That third check isn't a raise. It's money your normal budget doesn't need, and if you decide its job in advance, it can do more for you than almost any other payday of the year.

Why a third paycheck happens

A year has 52 weeks, so being paid every other week gives you 26 paychecks. Twelve months of two paychecks only uses 24 of them. The two left over land in whichever months happen to contain three of your paydays. In some years, depending on where your first payday falls, there are even 27 paychecks and three "three-paycheck months".

For example, if you're paid every other Friday starting 8 January 2027, your three-paycheck months in 2027 are April and October. If your first payday is Friday 1 January 2027 instead, you get three paychecks in January, July and December, and 27 paychecks in total. Your dates will differ, so check your own calendar.

Step 1: Find your three-paycheck months

Take your next payday and count forward 14 days at a time through the next twelve months. Circle any month with three paydays. It takes five minutes and turns a "surprise" into a planned event. Write those months in your calendar or at the top of your budget so you see them coming.

Step 2: Build your monthly budget on two paychecks only

This is the key to making the third check feel like extra money. Budget every month as if you get exactly two paychecks. All your regular bills, groceries, transport and savings must fit inside those two. Then, in the months with a third paycheck, nothing in your normal budget depends on it, so it's completely free to assign.

Here's an example for someone who takes home $1,800 every two weeks. Two paychecks give a monthly budget of $3,600:

Step 3: Check that your budget really is covered

Before spending the extra check, make sure the current month is actually on track. If you're behind on a bill, overdrawn, or you used a credit card to cover last month's groceries, fix that first. A third paycheck that goes to catching up is still a good use; it just means your two-paycheck budget needs adjusting so it doesn't happen again.

Step 4: Give the third paycheck a job

Decide the split before the money arrives. A common guideline is to put most of it towards your biggest current goal and keep a small part for something enjoyable, so the plan feels rewarding and you stick with it. Here's one way to split an $1,800 third paycheck:

With two third paychecks a year, this person would add $1,200 to their emergency fund and pay an extra $1,000 off debt, without changing their everyday budget at all.

Good jobs for a third paycheck

Start or top up an emergency fund

If you don't have savings for surprises yet, this is usually the best first job. Even one third paycheck can get you a solid starter cushion. Our guide on how to build an emergency fund explains how much to aim for.

Pay down high-interest debt

An extra payment on a credit card goes entirely to the balance, because your minimum is already covered by the regular budget. Pick one debt and put the whole debt portion on it.

Fill your sinking funds

Money set aside for known future costs, like the holidays, school supplies or the car insurance renewal, means those months won't squeeze your budget. A third paycheck in spring or autumn is perfect for this.

Get one month ahead

If you often feel like you're living paycheck to paycheck, put the third check into a separate "next month" account. Do this a couple of times and you can start paying this month's bills with last month's money, which makes every payday calmer.

Invest or save for a bigger goal

Once you have an emergency fund and no high-interest debt, the third paycheck can go towards retirement savings, a house deposit or another long-term goal.

Mistakes to avoid

Treating it as a bonus to spend. Without a plan, the extra money tends to disappear into small purchases. Assign it before payday.

Building your budget on three paychecks. If you average your income over the year (26 ÷ 12 ≈ 2.17 paychecks a month), every two-paycheck month will come up short. Budget on two.

Forgetting the timing. Some months with three paydays have a payday on the last day or two of the month. Make sure your bills for that month aren't due before the money arrives.

Ignoring weekly pay. If you're paid weekly, you get the same effect: four months a year usually have five paychecks. The same plan works; see how to budget a weekly paycheck.

Tip: write your plan for the third paycheck in your budget the month before it arrives, with exact amounts. When the money lands, move it within 24 hours. The less time it sits in your checking account, the more likely it is to do its job.
