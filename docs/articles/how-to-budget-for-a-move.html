How to Budget for a Move: Moving Costs, a Worked Example and a Savings Plan

Updated 2026-11-14 · 6 min read

Most people budget for the truck and forget everything else: the deposit, the first month's rent paid before the old deposit comes back, the cleaning, the takeout on box-filled evenings. A move is really two budgets: a one-off moving budget, and a new monthly budget for the new place. Plan both and moving day stops being a money shock.

1. Pick your moving date and count the months

Start with the date you need to be out of your current home. The number of months until then decides how much you need to save each month. Even six weeks of warning is enough to make a plan; three to six months makes it much easier.

Check your current lease for the notice period and any end-of-lease cleaning or repair rules. Missing a notice deadline can mean paying an extra month's rent, which is often the single biggest avoidable moving cost.

2. List every one-off cost

Walk through the move from packing to the first week in the new place and write down anything that costs money. Get real quotes or prices where you can instead of guessing. Typical lines:

  • Getting there: truck or van rental, fuel, mileage charges, parking, or a moving company quote.
  • Help: paid movers or helpers by the hour, or food for friends who help.
  • Supplies: boxes, tape, bubble wrap, mattress bags.
  • New home upfront: security deposit, first month's rent, application or admin fees.
  • Setup: utility connection fees, internet installation, new keys or locks.
  • Leaving well: cleaning the old place, small repairs, rubbish removal.
  • The first week: takeout, a few household basics, curtains, a shower curtain, light bulbs.

3. Worked example: a local move from a two-bedroom flat

Here is an example budget for a same-city move with a rental truck and two hired helpers. Your numbers will be different, but the categories are a good checklist.

CostNotesAmount
Truck rentalOne day$150
Fuel and mileageTwo trips$60
Moving help2 helpers × 4 hours × $40$320
Boxes and suppliesBoxes, tape, wrap$90
Security depositOne month's rent$1,400
First month's rentPaid at signing$1,400
Utility and internet setupConnection fees$75
Cleaning the old placeEnd-of-lease clean$120
First week extrasTakeout and basics$185
Subtotal$3,800
Buffer (10%)For surprises$380
Total to plan for$4,180

Notice that the truck, help and supplies together are only $620. The deposit and first month's rent are $2,800, almost three quarters of the subtotal. That is why moves feel so expensive: the big costs are not "moving" costs at all, they are the new home's upfront payments.

A 10% buffer is a common guideline, not a rule. If you are moving a long distance or using a moving company for the first time, a bigger buffer is sensible, because quotes can change with the final weight or extra stairs.

4. Don't count your old deposit until it arrives

You may get your old security deposit back, but usually after you have moved out and the landlord has checked the property. That can take weeks. Plan as if you must pay the new deposit and rent from your own savings, and treat the old deposit as money that refills your emergency fund or savings once it actually lands in your account.

5. Turn the total into a monthly savings target

Divide the total by the months you have left. In the example, with four months to go:

$4,180 ÷ 4 = $1,045 a month.

If that number is too big for your budget, you have a few levers:

  • Move the date if your lease allows it, to get more months to save.
  • Cut the help costs: ask friends, move small items yourself over several trips, or get free boxes from local shops.
  • Sell before you move: every piece of furniture you sell is money in and one less thing to carry.
  • Ask about the deposit: some landlords accept the deposit in parts. Get any agreement in writing.

Keep the moving money in a separate savings account or pot so it doesn't mix with everyday spending. This works exactly like a sinking fund: a fixed amount set aside each month for a cost you know is coming.

6. Rebuild your monthly budget for the new home

The one-off costs end after moving week. The new monthly costs don't. Compare your current monthly housing costs with the new place before you sign, not after:

Monthly costOld placeNew placeChange
Rent$1,250$1,400+$150
Utilities$140$170+$30
Commute$120$80−$40
Internet$60$60$0
Total$1,570$1,710+$140

In this example the new home costs $140 more each month, even though the rent alone went up $150, because the shorter commute saves $40. That $140 has to come from somewhere in your budget, every month. Decide now which category gives it up.

7. Track the move as it happens

Write down every moving expense as you pay it, with the planned amount next to it. After the move, compare the two. Whatever is left from the buffer goes straight back to savings. If you went over, you'll know exactly which line to budget higher for next time.

Tip: take dated photos of your old home on the day you leave and keep your cleaning receipt. It makes it easier to discuss the deposit with your landlord if there is a disagreement.

This guide is general information, not financial or legal advice. Rules on deposits and notice periods depend on where you live and on your lease.
