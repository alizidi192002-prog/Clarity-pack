Wedding Budget Breakdown by Percentage (With a Sample Budget)

Updated 2026-09-30 · 7 min read

The easiest way to plan a wedding budget is to decide the total first, then give each category a percentage. Here's a common starting split, a worked example, and how to keep track once deposits start.

A common starting split

These percentages are a guide many planners start from, not a rule. Venue and food together usually take about half.

How to adjust it to what matters to you

Pick your top two priorities and your bottom two. If photos matter most and flowers matter least, move 3–4% from flowers to photography. The total stays the same; only the split changes. Always keep the buffer — something always costs more than quoted.

The guest count is the biggest lever

Catering, venue size, invitations, favors and even rentals scale with guests. Divide your total by your guest count to see your cost per guest. Removing 10 guests often saves more than any single vendor negotiation.

Track quotes, deposits and due dates

Once you book vendors, the budget becomes a payment schedule. For each vendor, write the total, the deposit, the deposit due date and the balance due date. Compare every quote with what you planned for that category — if the venue comes in over, you know right away which category has to give.

Quick check: if your quotes add up to more than your total, don't wait. Cut guests or move money between categories before you sign the next contract.
