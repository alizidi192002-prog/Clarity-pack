How Much to Spend on Christmas Gifts: A Simple Budget Formula

Updated 2026-09-30 · 6 min read

There's no "right" amount to spend on Christmas. There's only the amount you can pay for without borrowing from January. Here's a simple way to find it and stick to it.

Step 1: Start from what you can save, not what you want to buy

Count the paychecks between now and mid-December. Decide how much you can set aside from each without touching rent, bills or your emergency fund. That total is your holiday budget. If you start in October, even a modest amount per paycheck adds up.

Example: 5 paychecks until December × $120 each = $600 holiday budget.

Step 2: Split the budget before you shop

Gifts are usually the biggest part, but food, decorations, travel, cards and events add up fast. A starting split many people find realistic:

AreaShareOf $600
Gifts60%$360
Food & hosting20%$120
Decor, cards & wrapping10%$60
Travel & events10%$60

Change the percentages to match your year. If you're travelling home, travel may need more than gifts.

Step 3: Give every person a number

List everyone you buy for and give each person a budget before you shop. Close family can get more; colleagues and friends can share a group gift or a small tradition like a Secret Santa limit.

PersonBudgetIdea
Mom$60Scarf

Partner$80Experience day
2 kids$120$60 each
Friends (4)$50Homemade treats

Step 4: Track as you go

Write down what you actually spend next to each budget, and mark gifts as bought and wrapped. The moment one person goes over, you know exactly where to cut elsewhere — before, not after.

Five ways to spend less without looking cheap

  • Start early and buy during sales instead of the last week.
  • Suggest a family Secret Santa with a price limit.
  • Give experiences or homemade food instead of objects.
  • Set a gift budget per household, not per person, for extended family.
  • Keep receipts in one place so returns in January are easy.
